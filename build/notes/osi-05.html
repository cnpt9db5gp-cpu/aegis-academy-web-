Social Engineering: Scope, Consent and Rules

Summary

This is the note about the boundary. Social engineering is the highest-value and
highest-liability activity in security work, and the difference between an engagement and an
incident is almost always a piece of paper.

The framing: with every other technique in this curriculum, the
worst case is a finding. With this one, the worst case is a person
being deceived. That changes the consent analysis completely.

The rule, stated once

  YOU MAY NOT MAKE A PERSON A PART OF AN ENGAGEMENT WITHOUT THEIR
  CONSENT.

  Not the receptionist. Not the "new IT contractor" they have never
  met. Not the vendor's support desk. Not the executive who is in on
  it and says yes.

  THEIR CONSENT, not your client's.

Why "the client said so" is not enough:

  THE TARGET IS NOT YOUR CLIENT.

  An employee of the target organisation is not the party to the
  contract. They are a data subject, a worker, and in many
  jurisdictions a person whose consent has not been sought for being
  deceived about a security matter.

  THE CONSEQUENCES, in rough order of severity:
    - employment consequences for a person who did nothing wrong
    - disciplinary or dismissal for a careless employee
    - a data breach notification triggered by your test
    - the client learning about it from the employee rather than
      from you
    - and in a small organisation, someone who is genuinely upset

  THE CLIENT AUTHORISES THE TEST. THE TARGET AUTHORISES BEING TESTED.
  Those are different permissions and only the first is one you get
  from a contract.

What consent has to cover, in writing

  1. WHO may be contacted, and who may not
     named individuals, a named team, or "anyone except <list>"
     the default should be "nobody" until the client names people.

  2. WHAT may be asked
     "what is your password" is different from "can you confirm your
     device is under management". One tests credential handling; the
     other tests process.

  3. WHAT may be PRETENDED
     "you are IT support" - is that permitted? Or only "you are
     conducting a security survey"? These are very different.

  4. WHAT may be OBTAINED, and what must be destroyed
     credentials collected must be destroyed immediately and the
     destruction attested. Credentials left around are a breach you
     created.

  5. WHAT HAPPENS AFTER
     who is told, when, and what they are told
     and what an employee is told if they "fall for it" - because
     many campaigns tell the target afterwards and that is its own
     decision to make, not yours.

  6. THE STOP
     a named person who can halt the campaign mid-flight, reachable,
     during the window.

The three tiers, and they are different activities

  TIER 1: PROCESS VERIFICATION (no person is deceived)
    "What is your name?" to the receptionist, to test whether the
    org publishes employee names.
    "I forgot my password" to IT, to test whether helpdesk staff will
    reset over the phone.
    Usually fine, usually low risk, and often needs no formal
    authorisation beyond the engagement letter.

  TIER 2: PRETEXTED CONTACT (a person is misled, briefly)
    Calling as a fake vendor to check whether the office is open at
    a claimed time.
    Needs named consent from the client. Low harm. Recoverable.

  TIER 3: CREDENTIAL OR ACCESS HARVESTING (a person is deceived and
          something is taken)
    A phishing email to real staff. A call collecting a password. A
    USB drop.
    Needs consent, a legal review, a data-handling plan, and a named
    individual accountable for it. High harm if it goes wrong. Often
    prohibited outright.

  THE COMMON MISTAKE: doing tier 3 because the engagement letter was
  generic enough not to prohibit it. "Not expressly forbidden" is not
  authorisation. See tcm-01's ROE template - if the activity is not
  listed, it is out.

What good engagement letters actually say

  WORDING THAT PERMITS NOTHING BY AMBIGUITY:

    "The tester may use any means necessary to identify and
     demonstrate vulnerabilities."

    ^ This is the clause that ends engagements. "Any means necessary"
      is read by a court as including anything, and it is read by an
      employee as including anything at all.

  WORDING THAT WORKS:

    "Permitted: network scanning of the in-scope ranges; service and
     version enumeration; exploitation of identified vulnerabilities
     to demonstrate access; authenticated testing using the supplied
     credentials.

     Prohibited: denial of service of any kind; social engineering of
     any kind, including phishing, pretexting, and telephone contact
     with staff; physical testing; testing of any third-party
     system; any contact with an employee who is not named in
     Appendix A.

     Appendix A lists the three staff members who have consented to
     being contacted, and the questions the tester may ask them."

  THE STRUCTURE: permitted / prohibited / appendix. Not "any means".

When the client asks for something you should decline

  THE REQUEST                                  WHY IT IS A PROBLEM
  -----------------------------------------    ------------------------------
  "phish our staff to see who clicks"          employment consequences for
                                               real people; personal data
                                               about individual performance

  "call the helpdesk and reset a password"     it works. it also creates a
                                               real credential issue

  "leave a USB stick in the car park"          this is not a finding, it is
                                               an intrusion, and it will
                                               trigger a physical
                                               security response

  "pretend to be a supplier and get in"        a real commercial
                                               relationship is forged

  "test the executive specifically"            the most identifiable
                                               target and the one with
                                               the most to lose

  WHAT TO SAY:
    "I can do this, with three conditions: named consent from the
     people who will be contacted, a written plan for what happens
     to their accounts, and an agreement that no individual's result
     is used in any performance or disciplinary process. If you want
     to measure click rate without that, I can run a phishing
     awareness module instead and it will tell you more."

  THAT ANSWER IS PROFESSIONAL AND IT IS RARELY REFUSED. The client
  usually did not know the first version had a data protection
  dimension.

The retrospective question

  AFTER ANY SOCIAL ENGINEERING ACTIVITY, these go in the report:

    - who was contacted, when, by whom, and with what pretext
    - what was asked and what was answered
    - what was obtained, and confirmation it was destroyed
    - the timeline of the campaign, to the minute
    - what the client should change, specifically
    - and: who inside the client knew, in advance, in writing

  THE LAST LINE IS the one that distinguishes a professional
  engagement from an incident. It is also the line that cannot be
  added retroactively.

Terminology

Term Meaning
Social engineering Influencing a person to act, using deception
Pretexting A false scenario to obtain information or access
Vishing / Smishing Social engineering by voice / by SMS
Consent The target's own agreement to be deceived
Authorisation The client's permission to conduct the test
Credential harvesting Collecting credentials through deception
Phishing simulation A phishing campaign run as an awareness control
Goad / tailgating Physical access via persuasion
Dumpster diving Searching discarded material; no person deceived
Vishing consent A separate, specific permission for voice contact
"Any means necessary" A clause that ends engagements
Data protection impact The legal dimension of targeting real staff

# Social engineering scope and consent template

> Fill every field. An unfilled field is an activity nobody
> authorised. (This is tcm-01's ROE template applied to the one
> category where the target, not the client, is the party at risk.)

## 1. The tiers in scope for this engagement
- [ ] Tier 1 - process verification (no person deceived)
      Permitted: ______________________________________________
- [ ] Tier 2 - pretexted contact (person misled briefly)
      Permitted: ______________________________________________
- [ ] Tier 3 - credential or access harvesting
      Permitted: ____________________ [ ] NONE. Default is NONE.

## 2. The prohibition, stated explicitly
Social engineering of any kind is PROHIBITED unless listed in
section 1. This includes:
  - phishing, smishing, vishing, and any message or call designed to
    obtain credentials, information, or access from a person
  - pretexting any employee not named in Appendix A
  - any contact with a third party (supplier, partner, customer)
  - any physical deception (tailgating, goad doors, USB drops)
  - impersonating any named individual

If an activity is not listed in section 1, it is OUT OF SCOPE.

## 3. Named individuals who have consented to contact
| Name | Role | Contacted by | Pretexts permitted | Questions permitted |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | |
| | | | | |

Appendix A is the ONLY list of people who may be contacted.
Nobody else, at any level, for any reason, including a
"just checking" call.

## 4. Data handling for anything obtained
- Credentials collected: destroyed within ______ minutes,
  destruction attested by: ______________________
- Retention: none. Written confirmation on the day.
- Any credential obtained must not be used for any purpose
  whatsoever, including "just to check".

## 5. Who knows, in advance
| Name | Role | Informed on | Method | Written confirmation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | |

If this section cannot be completed, the activity does not happen.
There is no partial version.

## 6. The stop
Named person who can halt the activity mid-flight:
  Name: ______________  Contact: ______________  Hours: ______

## 7. Afterwards
- [ ] The client is told what was done, in the report
- [ ] Any individual who was contacted is told, if the client
      decides to do so - this is the CLIENT's decision, not the
      tester's
- [ ] No individual's result is used in any performance review,
      disciplinary process, or personnel record
- [ ] The report states who knew, in advance, in writing
EOF
echo "written: 01-scope.md"

cat > 02-check.md <<'EOF'
#!/usr/bin/env bash
# Check a ROE for social-engineering ambiguity BEFORE the engagement.
# Grep for the phrases that end engagements.
F="${1:-../../docs/RULES-OF-ENGAGEMENT.md}"
[ -f "$F" ] || { echo "no ROE at $F"; exit 1; }

echo "=== the phrases that end engagements ==="
for p in "any means necessary" "all techniques" "without restriction" \
         "as necessary" "unspecified" "any method" "creative"; do
  if grep -qi "$p" "$F"; then
    echo "  FOUND: '$p'"
    echo "    ^ read this clause. 'any means necessary' is interpreted"
    echo "      by a court as unlimited, and by a nervous employee as"
    echo "      unlimited. Replace with an explicit permitted/prohibited list."
  fi
done
grep -qi "any means necessary" "$F" || echo "  none found (good)"

echo
echo "=== the required clauses, present or not ==="
for c in "social engineering" "phishing" "denial of service" "physical" \
         "third-party" "scope" "out of scope" "authorised" "authoriz" \
         "data handling" "destroy" "emergency contact" "stop"; do
  if grep -qi "$c" "$F"; then
    printf "  present: %s\n" "$c"
  else
    printf "  MISSING: %-22s <- add it\n" "$c"
  fi
done

echo
echo "=== the specific question: is social engineering PERMITTED? ==="
if grep -qiE "social engineering[^.]*(prohibit|not permitted|out of scope|excluded)" "$F"; then
  echo "  PROHIBITED. Good. Do not do it."
elif grep -qiE "social engineering[^.]*(permitted|authoris|authoriz|allowed)" "$F"; then
  echo "  PERMITTED somewhere. Read the sentence exactly. 'Permitted'"
  echo "  with no list of people, pretexts, and questions is not"
  echo "  permission - it is a liability."
else
  echo "  NOT MENTIONED. That means NOT PERMITTED."
  echo "  Silent clauses are read as prohibitions by everyone except"
  echo "  the tester."
fi

echo
echo "=== the Appendix A check ==="
if grep -qiE "appendix a|appendix 1|named individual" "$F"; then
  echo "  a named-individual appendix exists. Check it is not empty."
else
  echo "  NO named-individual appendix. Nobody may be contacted."
fi
EOF
chmod +x 02-check.sh
./02-check.sh ../../docs/RULES-OF-ENGAGEMENT.md 2>&1 | sed 's/^/  /' | tee 03-check.txt

Step 2 is the deliverable: a script that greps a ROE before the engagement for the phrases
that end engagements — "any means necessary", "all techniques", "without restriction" — and for
the required clauses that should be present but often aren't. It ends with the question that
matters most: if social engineering isn't mentioned at all, that means NOT PERMITTED, because
silent clauses are read as prohibitions by everyone except the tester.

01-scope.md is the tiered consent template, with Tier 3 defaulting to NONE and the
Appendix A rule stated absolutely: Appendix A is the only list of people who may be contacted —
nobody else, at any level, for any reason, including a "just checking" call.
