Mandatory Hygiene: Isolated Lab, Snapshots, and Scope

Summary

This is the least entertaining and most important lecture in Tier 0. Before you run a single tool
against a single target, you need three things in place: an isolated lab, a snapshot, and
a written scope.

Skip any of them and you risk one of three outcomes: scanning a network that is not yours,
destroying an environment you cannot rebuild, or losing weeks of work to a mistyped rm -rf.

The three non-negotiables

1. Isolated lab

Your attacker machine (Kali) and your targets (VMs) must be on a host-only network with no
route to your real LAN or the internet. Covered in kr-06.

# Verify before you scan anything
ip route | grep 192.168.56       # host-only subnet present
ip route | grep default          # your real gateway — different subnet

2. Snapshot before every destructive step

VBoxManage snapshot vuln01 take "pristine"
VBoxManage snapshot vuln01 list
VBoxManage snapshot vuln01 restore "pristine"

Snapshot cadence that actually works:

Snapshot When
pristine immediately after import, before touching anything
mid-exercise before anything irreversible (mass changes, format, partition work)
solved after a successful run, as your reference solution

3. Written scope

Even against your own lab, write it down. The habit is the point.

mkdir -p ~/aegis/engagements
cat > ~/aegis/engagements/$(date +%Y%m%d-%H%M%S)-scope.md <<'EOF'
# Engagement Scope

**Authorisation:** self-directed practice. No third-party systems in scope.
**Date:** (today)
**Tester:** nwpcs @ localhost

## In scope
- 192.168.56.0/24  (host-only lab network)
- vuln01  .101  Metasploitable3
- vuln02  .102  DVWA / Juice Shop
- vuln03  .103  Misconfigured Ubuntu (privesc)

## Out of scope
- Any address outside 192.168.56.0/24
- Denial of service of any kind
- Social engineering, phishing, physical access
- Data destruction or disk wiping

## Permitted techniques
- Scanning, enumeration, exploitation, post-exploitation, pivoting (within the range)
- Credential recovery against hashes obtained from in-scope targets only

## Emergency contact
- Stop everything; restore the nearest snapshot.
EOF

Why write it for a self-practice engagement? Because in three months you will be running
something against something else, and the muscle memory of "scope first" is what keeps you inside
the lines.

Operational hygiene that saves you

# --- destructive command guardrails ---
# Always know where you are and what you are pointing at
pwd
ls -la
# Use a two-step delete for anything that matters
mkdir -p /tmp/trash && mv important-stuff /tmp/trash/    # reversible
rm -rf /tmp/trash/                                        # now irreversible, and safe

# Make the dangerous ones harder to type by accident
alias rm='rm -i'
alias cp='cp -i'
alias mv='mv -i'

# --- history hygiene (both for privacy and for not leaving trails on yourself) ---
export HISTCONTROL=ignorebothspace    # commands starting with a space are not recorded
export HISTSIZE=50000
export HISTFILESIZE=200000

# --- never commit secrets or captures ---
cd ~/Projects/aegis-academy
git check-ignore -v labs/foo.pcap

# --- evidence hygiene ---
mkdir -p ~/aegis/evidence && chmod 700 ~/aegis/evidence
sha256sum capture.pcap | tee capture.pcap.sha256

The snapshot discipline that actually prevents disaster

#!/usr/bin/env bash
# snap.sh - one command snapshot discipline
set -euo pipefail
VM="${1:?usage: ./snap.sh <vmname> [label]}"
LABEL="${2:-manual-$(date +%H%M%S)}"
VBoxManage snapshot "$VM" take "$LABEL" --description "$LABEL"
echo "[+] snapshot '$LABEL' taken on $VM"
VBoxManage snapshot "$VM" list | tail -5

# use it
./snap.sh vuln01 pristine
./snap.sh vuln01 before-mitigation-test
./snap.sh vuln01 solved-buffer-overflow

Terminology

Term Meaning
Rules of Engagement (RoE) The document defining what is permitted
Scope The specific systems, IPs, techniques and time window authorised
Isolation Network separation preventing accidental access outside scope
Snapshot Frozen VM state; the cheapest rollback available
Destructive operation Anything that cannot be undone (format, rm -rf, partition)
Chain of custody Documented record of who handled evidence, and when
Evidence integrity Cryptographic hash proving evidence was not altered
Scope creep The gradual expansion of testing beyond authorisation

Field lab

Objective: establish the full hygiene baseline on your own machine, and verify your snapshot
recovery actually works before you need it.

mkdir -p ~/aegis/labs/doc-02 && cd ~/aegis/labs/doc-02

# --- 1. write the scope document (do not skip - it is the deliverable) ---
mkdir -p ~/aegis/engagements
SCOPE=~/aegis/engagements/$(date +%Y%m%d)-scope.md
cat > "$SCOPE" <<'EOF'
# Engagement Scope — AEGIS practice range
Authorisation: self-directed. No third-party systems.
IN:  192.168.56.0/24 (host-only), localhost, and my own VMs
OUT: everything else; no DoS; no social engineering; no destruction
EOF
cat "$SCOPE" | tee 01-scope.txt

# --- 2. build the snapshot helper and USE it ---
cat > snap.sh <<'SCRIPT'
#!/usr/bin/env bash
set -euo pipefail
VM="${1:?usage: ./snap.sh <vmname> [label]}"
LABEL="${2:-manual-$(date +%H%M%S)}"
VBoxManage snapshot "$VM" take "$LABEL" --description "$LABEL" >/dev/null
echo "[+] snapshot '$LABEL' taken on $VM"
SCRIPT
chmod +x snap.sh
VBoxManage list runningvms

# --- 3. PROVE snapshot recovery works (this is the step everyone skips) ---
# If you have a lab VM:
VM=vuln01
./snap.sh $VM hygiene-test
# break something deliberately inside the VM (create a file, install nothing)
# then restore and confirm the break is gone
VBoxManage snapshot $VM list | tail -6

# --- 4. environment hardening ---
cat >> ~/.bashrc <<'EOF'

# --- AEGIS hygiene ---
export HISTCONTROL=ignorebothspace
export HISTSIZE=50000
export HISTFILESIZE=200000
alias rm='rm -i'
alias cp='cp -i'
alias mv='mv -i'
alias netstat-tools='ss -tulpn'
EOF
# (remove the aliases if you dislike being interrupted - the point is being deliberate)

# --- 5. evidence directory with restricted permissions ---
mkdir -p ~/aegis/evidence && chmod 700 ~/aegis/evidence
ls -ld ~/aegis/evidence

# --- 6. verify your repo ignores captures ---
cd ~/Projects/aegis-academy
git check-ignore -v lab.pcap transcripts/foo.vtt 2>/dev/null | tee /tmp/ig.txt
git ls-files | grep -E '\.(pcap|mem|raw|dmp)$' && echo "!! TRACKED EVIDENCE - fix this" \
  || echo "good: no evidence tracked in git"

# --- 7. self-audit: what is this machine listening on, really? ---
{
  echo "=== listening sockets ==="
  ss -tulpn
  echo
  echo "=== anything bound to 0.0.0.0 (world-reachable) is a finding ==="
  ss -tulpn | awk '$5 ~ /0\.0\.0\.0|\*/ {print "  EXPOSED: " $1, $5, $7}'
} | tee 02-exposure-audit.txt

# --- 8. the reversible-delete pattern ---
mkdir -p /tmp/trash-demo && echo "important" > /tmp/trash-demo/file.txt
mkdir -p /tmp/trash && mv /tmp/trash-demo /tmp/trash/    # reversible!
ls /tmp/trash/
rm -rf /tmp/trash/                                       # now irreversible, and safe

Step 7 is a real finding about your own machine. Anything bound to 0.0.0.0 is reachable
from your whole network. On a laptop that is frequently a real problem, and it is precisely the
misconfiguration you will look for in Tier 5.

Record: whether you have any world-reachable listener. Fix it now — kill the process or bind
it to 127.0.0.1.

Self-test

  2. Why is a written scope valuable even for a self-practice engagement?
  3. What does HISTCONTROL=ignorebothspace do, and why is it useful?
  4. Your lab VM will not boot after an experiment. Which two recovery mechanisms exist, and which

  5. ss -tulpn shows a service on 0.0.0.0:8080. What is the risk, and how do you fix it?
