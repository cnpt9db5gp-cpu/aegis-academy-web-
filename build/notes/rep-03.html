Risk Rating, Business Impact and Prioritisation

Summary

Severity is a judgement about business impact, not a score. The lecture's framing is that a
CVSS score measures the technical severity of a class of bug, while a risk rating measures the
damage a specific instance of it would do to a specific organisation — and those are different
numbers.

The framing: severity is a sentence about the business, and the
number is at the end of it. A rating with no prerequisite and no
business impact is a number in search of a meaning.

Companion to cs161-01's threat model and tcm-07. This note is
the rating discipline specifically.

The two numbers, and what each measures

  CVSS (and its successors)
    measures the TECHNICAL SEVERITY OF A CLASS OF BUG
    Attack Vector, Attack Complexity, Privileges Required,
    User Interaction, Scope, Confidentiality/Integrity/Availability

    Useful for: consistent internal language, prioritising a bug
                backlog, comparing two instances of the same CVE
    NOT useful for: deciding what your organisation should fix first
    WHY: it does not know whether you have a database with
         customer data behind that endpoint, whether the exploit is
         one HTTP request away, or whether a WAF is in the way.

  RISK RATING (yours)
    measures THE DAMAGE THIS BUG WOULD DO HERE, TO THIS ORGANISATION
    Likelihood (exploitability in this context)
    × Impact (what it would cost this business)
    with the prerequisite stated, so the reader can disagree

  THE RELATIONSHIP: a Critical CVSS can be a Medium finding here, and
  a Medium CVSS can be a Critical finding here. Both happen weekly.

Likelihood: five factors, not one

  1. REACHABILITY
     Is there a path from the internet, from an employee, from a
     phishing email? Or is it only reachable after five other
     compromises?

  2. AUTHENTICATION
     Unauthenticated > any valid account > a specific account >
     a privileged account > physical access with the right equipment.

  3. USER INTERACTION
     None > one click on a link you sent > opening a document you
     were sent > entering credentials you were asked for.

  4. COMPLEXITY
     One HTTP request > a specific payload > a race condition > a
     chain of three other vulnerabilities.

  5. DETECTION AND MITIGATION PRESENT
     Does a WAF block it? Is the segment isolated? Would the SOC
     see an attempt? A finding with a reliable mitigation in place
     is lower likelihood - and the mitigation is itself a finding if
     it is the only thing standing there.

  AND THE ONE PEOPLE FORGET:
  6. EXPLOITABILITY TRACK RECORD
     Is there a public exploit? Is it in Metasploit? Is it in a
     scanner's default module set? A public exploit changes
     likelihood more than anything else on this list.

Impact: what it costs, in the organisation's language

  CONFIDENTIALITY
    what data, how much, how sensitive
    "the customer database, 41,882 records including partial card
     data" is an impact statement.
    "data may be exposed" is not.

  INTEGRITY
    what can be changed, by whom, and what does a wrong change do
    "an unauthenticated user can change a customer's delivery
     address" is very different from "an unauthenticated user can
     change a customer's delivery address, which affects billing
     and, for address-change fraud, is a regulated notification in
     [jurisdiction]".

  AVAILABILITY
    how long, how recoverable, and who notices
    "the service is unavailable until someone restarts it" is very
     different from "the service is unavailable and the loss is
     4 hours of trading, £X, with a [regulator] notification
     requirement".

  THE TEST: could you write the impact sentence with a NUMBER in it,
  and would that number mean something to someone's finance team?
  If not, it is not an impact statement yet.

The matrix, and why the prerequisite is the real answer

                      IMPACT
                  low        high
             +-----------+-----------+
   LIKELY    |  Medium   |  CRITICAL |
   (one req) |           |           |
             +-----------+-----------+
   POSSIBLE  |  Low      |  Medium   |
   (a chain) |           |           |
             +-----------+-----------+
   UNLIKELY  |  Info     |  Low      |
   (needs X) |           |           |
             +-----------+-----------+

  THE PREREQUISITE IS WHERE THE MATRIX IS APPLIED.
  Every cell is conditional on "given that <prerequisite>".
  Without that clause, the matrix has no meaning, because the reader
  cannot tell whether their environment has the prerequisite.

The two errors, and the corrective for each

  ERROR 1: INFLATED
    "Critical" for something with no reachable path.
    WHY IT HAPPENS: the CVSS was 9.8 and nobody checked whether the
    port was open.
    THE CORRECTIVE: answer, in the finding, "what must be true for
    this to be exploited?" If the answer is "the attacker must
    already be domain administrator", the finding is Medium at best,
    and probably an aggregation of other findings.

  ERROR 2: DEFLATED
    "Informational" for something the client will not fix.
    WHY IT HAPPENS: the tester could not demonstrate it.
    THE CORRECTIVE: severity is about the business, not about your
    ability to demonstrate it. "The backup files are world-readable
    and contain the full user database" is Critical whether or not
    you read them - and saying "I did not read them" is a strength,
    not a weakness.

  THE SAME CORRECTIVE FOR BOTH:
    state the prerequisite explicitly, and let the reader apply the
    matrix themselves. A finding that says "exploitable by any
    authenticated user, no user interaction" can be rated correctly
    by anyone in five seconds. A finding that says "Critical" can be
    rated correctly by nobody.

#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""Score and prioritise. The NUMBER is the last thing; the
prerequisite column is the first."""
FINDINGS = [
 dict(id="F-01", title="Unauthenticated RCE on WEB-01 -> customer DB",
      reach="internal network", auth="none", interaction="none",
      complexity="one HTTP request", exploit_public=True,
      detect=False, impact_data="customer records, partial card data",
      impact_int="", impact_avail=""),
 dict(id="F-02", title="SMB signing not required on 34 hosts",
      reach="internal network", auth="any valid logon (relayed)",
      interaction="one click on a link", complexity="one packet",
      exploit_public=True, detect=False, impact_data="any account on the LAN",
      impact_int="", impact_avail=""),
 dict(id="F-03", title="Kernel privesc, needs local admin first",
      reach="already an admin", auth="administrator", interaction="none",
      complexity="local exploit, offsets required", exploit_public=False,
      detect=True, impact_data="unrestricted host access",
      impact_int="", impact_avail=""),
 dict(id="F-04", title="HTTP headers missing CSP and HSTS",
      reach="any client", auth="none", interaction="none",
      complexity="none", exploit_public=False, detect=True,
      impact_data="none directly", impact_int="none directly",
      impact_avail="none directly"),
]

def likelihood(f):
    s = 0
    s += {"internet": 4, "internal network": 3, "any client": 2,
          "already an admin": 0}.get(f["reach"], 1)
    s += {"none": 3, "any valid logon (relayed)": 2, "administrator": 0,
          "specific account": 1}.get(f["auth"], 1)
    s += {"none": 2, "one click on a link": 1, "enter credentials": 0,
          "open a document": 0}.get(f["interaction"], 1)
    s += {"one HTTP request": 3, "one packet": 3, "local exploit, offsets required": 0,
          "none": 3}.get(f["complexity"], 1)
    s += 2 if f["exploit_public"] else 0
    s -= 1 if f["detect"] else 0
    return max(s, 0)

def impact(f):
    s = 0
    for v in (f["impact_data"], f["impact_int"], f["impact_avail"]):
        t = v.lower()
        if any(k in t for k in ("none", "")): s -= 1
        elif any(k in t for k in ("customer", "card", "password",
                                  "any account", "unrestricted")): s += 2
        else: s += 1
    return max(s, 0)

MATRIX = {  # (likelihood, impact) -> rating
 (0,0):"Informational",(0,1):"Low",(0,2):"Low",
 (1,0):"Low",         (1,1):"Medium",(1,2):"Medium",
 (2,0):"Low",         (2,1):"Medium",(2,2):"High",
 (3,0):"Medium",      (3,1):"High",  (3,2):"Critical",
 (4,0):"Medium",      (4,1):"High",  (4,2):"Critical",
}

print(f"  {'ID':<5} {'L':<3} {'I':<3} {'RATING':<14} TITLE")
print("  " + "-"*84)
rows = []
for f in FINDINGS:
    L, I = likelihood(f), impact(f)
    rating = MATRIX.get((min(L,4), min(I,2)), "Medium")
    rows.append((L+I, L, I, rating, f))
for tot, L, I, rating, f in sorted(rows, reverse=True):
    print(f"  {f['id']:<5} {L:<3} {I:<3} {rating:<14} {f['title']}")

print()
print("  THE PREREQUISITE, WHICH IS WHAT THE NUMBER IS CONDITIONAL ON:")
for _, L, I, rating, f in sorted(rows, reverse=True):
    print(f"  {f['id']}: {rating}")
    print(f"    given: reachable from the {f['reach']}, requires {f['auth']},")
    print(f"    user interaction: {f['interaction']}, complexity: {f['complexity']}")
    print(f"    public exploit: {'yes' if f['exploit_public'] else 'no'}, "
          f"detectability: {'yes' if f['detect'] else 'no'}")
print()
print("  NOTE F-03 and F-04. A kernel privesc that needs admin first is")
print("  rated below a signing misconfiguration, because the signing")
print("  misconfiguration is reachable by every user on the LAN with one")
print("  click. That is usually right, and it is never what a CVSS")
print("  score would have told you.")
print()
print("  NOTE F-04. No direct impact on any axis, so it scores Low on")
print("  both and lands Informational - and it is STILL worth fixing,")
print("  because it is the control that limits F-01 and F-02. Say that")
print("  in the finding rather than pretending it is a High.")

The severity sentence

  WRITE IT AS A SENTENCE WITH THE NUMBER AT THE END.

  GOOD  "Any authenticated user on the internal network can relay an
         authentication to any of 34 file servers and read every file
         they can read. No user interaction beyond opening a link the
         attacker sends. A public exploit exists. CRITICAL."

  BAD   "SMB signing issue. Severity: High. CVSS: 8.1."

  WHY THE FIRST IS BETTER:
    it contains the reachability, the authentication requirement, the
    user interaction, the exploit availability, and the impact - all
    of them - and a reader who disagrees with "CRITICAL" can identify
    exactly which factor they disagree with. A reader who disagrees
    with a bare number has nothing to push back on.
