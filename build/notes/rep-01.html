Engagement Structure: Scoping, Rules of Engagement, Reporting

Summary

This note is the template layer over tcm-01 and osi-05: the four documents that define an
engagement, what each one decides, and the order they have to exist in.

The framing: these documents are not paperwork. They are the
control surface. Every decision you make during an engagement was
either authorised here or was a mistake, and the only defence in
either case is the document.

Companion to tcm-01 (the operational doctrine) and osi-05 (the
consent boundary). This note is the structure.

The four documents

  1. ENGAGEMENT LETTER / SOW   the commercial contract. What is
                               being bought, for how much, by when.
                               Signed by both parties. It DEFERS to
                               the ROE on technical specifics.

  2. RULES OF ENGAGEMENT (ROE) the technical authorisation. What may
                               be touched, when, how hard, and what is
                               forbidden. Signed by the client's
                               authorised representative, who has the
                               authority to grant it.
                               THIS IS THE DOCUMENT THAT MATTERS.

  3. SCOPE STATEMENT            part of the ROE, often its own annex.
                               The exact addresses, domains,
                               hostnames, and wireless SSIDs, in and
                               out. Written as lists, not descriptions.

  4. DELIVERABLES AND SCHEDULE  what is produced, in what format, by
                               when, and what retest is included.

  THE ORDER THEY MUST EXIST IN
    letter  ->  ROE  ->  scope  ->  start working
    ^ and the ROE cannot be started without the signature of someone
      who can actually grant it, verified, not assumed.

Who can sign

  THE TEST: does this person have the AUTHORITY to permit what the
  test will do to a system they may not own?

  USUALLY YES
    the CISO, the security manager, the IT director, the head of IT
    the legal counsel who has consulted the client

  USUALLY NO
    an engineer on the team whose systems will be affected
    a team lead with no authority over the budget or the risk
    the vendor's account manager, who sells but cannot grant

  AND THE FAILURE MODE
    someone with authority signs for something they do not control.
    The systems group finds out at 02:00 and the engagement is over.
    This is why the ROE names the CLIENT'S technical contact AND the
    person who can stop the test - and why you call them BEFORE the
    window, not during it.

  VERIFY, DON'T ASSUME
    ask for the authorisation in writing, reference it in your diary,
    and confirm the person has the role. A signed email from a
    "Head of Infrastructure" is not the same as the CISO if the
    systems are regulated or customer-facing.

cat > 00-STRUCTURE.md <<'EOF'
# The engagement document set

```text
  ENGAGEMENT LETTER / SOW
    Parties and authority
    Objective (in business terms, not techniques)
    Deliverables and dates
    Fees
    Confidentiality and data handling
    Liability
    "The tester is bound by the Rules of Engagement in Annex A"

  RULES OF ENGAGEMENT
    1. Authorisation (who grants it, and their authority)
    2. Scope: in (addresses/domains/SSIDs) and out (everything else)
    3. Time window, with timezone
    4. Permitted techniques (a list)
    5. Prohibited techniques (a list)
    6. Data handling: what may be read, what must be destroyed
    7. Contacts: technical, and the person who can stop the test
    8. Deliverables and retest
    9. Signatures

  SCOPE ANNEX
    A table of in-scope targets. ADDRESSES, not descriptions.
    "the internal network" is not a scope. "192.168.56.0/24 and
     10.10.10.0/24" is a scope.

  TESTING HOURS
    Sometimes different from the window. A window is when you MAY
    test; hours are when you SHOULD. An overnight scan of a business
    network is within the window and outside the hours, and only one
    of those is a finding.
```
EOF

cat > 01-scope-annex.md <<'EOF'
# Scope annex — TEMPLATE

> The single most common source of a ruined engagement is a scope
> written as a description rather than a list. Fill every cell.

## In scope

| Target | Type | Notes | Start | End |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 192.168.56.0/24 | network | host-only lab range | | |
| lab.internal | domain | AD forest | | |
| https://app.lab.internal | application | | | |
| SSID: AEGIS-LAB | wireless | | | |

## Explicitly OUT of scope

| Target | Reason |
|---|---|
| Everything not listed above | default deny |
| 10.0.0.0/8 and 172.16.0.0/12 | not owned by the client |
| Any public internet host | not in this engagement |
| Third-party SaaS, CDN, and cloud | not owned by the client |
| Physical locations | not in this methodology |
| Staff social engineering | prohibited unless named in Appendix A |
| Denial of service | PROHIBITED |
| The client's customers | not testable by this engagement |

## Credentials supplied

| Account | Permitted for |
|---|---|
| LAB\alice (domain user) | network enumeration, AD reconnaissance |
| LAB\svc-test (service) | authenticated web testing only |
| app_test (application) | the application only; no OS access |

Note explicitly: "credentials may be used for authentication. They may
NOT be used for post-exploitation outside the in-scope targets, and
passwords found on one host may not be tried on another."

## Rate limits and windows

| Activity | Limit | Window |
|---|---|---|
| Scanning | 1000 pps maximum | 09:00–17:00 local |
| Authentication attempts | 5 per account per hour | as above |
| Exploitation | none outside 09:00–22:00 | as above |
| Out-of-hours | nothing without written approval | — |

## Test window

| | |
|---|---|
| Start | (date, time, timezone) |
| End | (date, time, timezone) |
| Daily hours | 09:00–17:00, Mon–Fri |
| Excluded dates | public holidays, change freezes, and <dates> |
| Outside the window | all activity stops immediately |

## Emergency procedure

If the test causes an outage or a security incident:
1. Stop testing immediately.
2. Call the technical contact: __________________ (24h during window)
3. Call the stop authority: __________________
4. Do not attempt to remediate. That is the client's change process.

The tester will not restart until the client says so, in writing.

EOF
echo "written: 01-scope-annex.md"

cat > 02-preflight.sh <<'EOF'

!/usr/bin/env bash

The pre-engagement check. Run it the week before. Nothing here is

optional and nothing here can be done on the day.

echo "=============================================================="
echo " PRE-ENGAGEMENT CHECK"
echo "=============================================================="
f=0
chk() { if [ "$1" = "y" ]; then printf " [ok] %s\n" "$2"; else printf " [MISS] %s\n" "$2"; f=1; fi; }

echo
echo "--- authorisation ---"
chk "${AUTH_SIGNED:-n}" "ROE signed by the client's authorised representative"
chk "${AUTH_AUTHORITY_VERIFIED:-n}" "Signer's authority to grant this tested"
chk "${AUTH_WRITTEN:-n}" "Authorisation exists in writing, not verbally"
echo
echo "--- scope ---"
chk "${SCOPE_ADDRESSES:-n}" "In-scope targets written as addresses, not descriptions"
chk "${SCOPE_OUTLIST:-n}" "Out-of-scope list is explicit and non-empty"
chk "${SCOPE_CREDENTIALS:-n}" "Credentials supplied and their permitted use stated"
chk "${SCOPE_RATELIMITS:-n}" "Rate limits stated"
echo
echo "--- timing ---"
chk "${WINDOW_SET:-n}" "Start and end times, with timezone"
chk "${WINDOW_HOURS:-n}" "Daily testing hours stated"
chk "${WINDOW_EXCLUDED:-n}" "Excluded dates (holidays, change freezes) stated"
echo
echo "--- communications ---"
chk "${CONTACT_PRIMARY:-n}" "Technical contact named, with a number"
chk "${CONTACT_CALLTESTED:-n}" "Contact CALLED BEFORE the window, not during"
chk "${CONTACT_STOP:-n}" "One named person who can halt the test"
echo
echo "--- environment ---"
chk "${IP_WHITELISTED:-n}" "Source IPs whitelisted with the client's IDS"
chk "${IP_RECORDED:-n}" "Source IPs recorded in the diary"
chk "${LOGGING:-n}" "Logging enabled, so the client's IDS sees the traffic"
echo
echo "--- deliverable ---"
chk "${DELIVERABLES_FORMAT:-n}" "Report format agreed"
chk "${DELIVERABLES_DATE:-n}" "Delivery date agreed"
chk "${DELIVERABLES_RETEST:-n}" "Retest included or explicitly excluded"
echo
echo "--- personal preparation ---"
chk "${PREP_NOTES:-n}" "Notes template, tooling, and ROE to hand"
chk "${PREP_DIARY:-n}" "Engagement diary created (append-only)"
chk "${PREP_STOPCONDITIONS:-n}" "Stop conditions written BEFORE the test"
chk "${PREP_EXITPLAN:-n}" "Plan for leaving cleanly: remove tools, verify, notify"
echo
if [ "$f" -eq 0 ]; then
echo " All items present. The engagement can start."
else
echo " Items missing. Resolve them before the window opens."
echo " Every one of them has ended a real engagement."
fi
exit $f
EOF
chmod +x 02-preflight.sh

````bash
cat > 03-stop.md <<'EOF'
# Stop conditions — written before the engagement starts

```text
  I WILL STOP IMMEDIATELY AND NOT RESUME WITHOUT WRITTEN INSTRUCTION IF:

  1. A service I am testing becomes unavailable and I cannot explain
     why within 5 minutes.
  2. Any data appears to have been modified, encrypted, or deleted.
  3. I encounter a system, network, or organisation I was not told
     about - especially one that looks like a different client.
  4. I obtain access to credential material I was not expecting,
     on a system outside the in-scope list.
  5. I am asked to stop by anyone at the client.
  6. Any activity appears to be reaching something outside the scope
     annex, even if I did not deliberately cause it.
  7. My own tooling behaves in a way I did not expect and could cause
     damage.

  WHEN I STOP:
    - I will not attempt to fix anything. That is the client's change
      process, their backups, and their outage, not mine.
    - I will call the technical contact, and if unanswered within 15
      minutes, the stop authority.
    - I will record the time, what I was doing, and what happened.
    - I will wait for written instruction before resuming.
    - If I caused it, I will say so, immediately, without
      embellishment. A tester who says "I don't know what happened"
      after causing it has a much worse problem than one who says
      "I ran X at 14:22 and the service went down at 14:23."

  THE MAXIMUM
    One exploit at a time against any single host. No parallel
    exploitation. No persistence testing without explicit permission.

  WHAT I WILL NOT DO, EVER, WITHOUT WRITTEN PERMISSION IN ADVANCE:
    - denial of service, of any kind, even a "small" one
    - modifying, deleting, or encrypting any data
    - leaving a persistence mechanism on a host
    - moving data off a host other than to prove impact on a named
      finding
    - contacting any third-party system
    - any social engineering of any person

EOF
echo "written: 03-stop.md"

cat > 04-diary.sh <<'EOF'

!/usr/bin/env bash

An append-only engagement diary. Not editable. Not truncatable.

D="${1:-diary.txt}"
touch "$D"; chmod 0444 "$D" 2>/dev/null
log() {
chmod 0644 "$D" 2>/dev/null
printf '%s %s\n' "$(date -u +%Y-%m-%dT%H:%M:%SZ)" "$*" >> "$D"
chmod 0444 "$D" 2>/dev/null
}

unset it so the caller cannot log through it

unset -f log

--- the entry discipline ---

cat <<'NOTE'
WHAT EVERY ENTRY CONTAINS
- a UTC timestamp (with the timezone, always)
- what you did
- on which target
- with which credential (never the credential itself)
- and, for anything disruptive or surprising, what you think
happened and who you told

WHAT IS NOT IN THE DIARY
- passwords, tokens, or hashes. Ever. A diary is a document the
client reads.
- speculation presented as fact. Write "X appears to be" and
separate it from what you observed.

WHY IT IS APPEND-ONLY AND READ-ONLY
Because its value is that it is complete. A diary with a gap, or a
diary you can edit, is worth nothing in a dispute - and the dispute
is exactly when you will want it.

EXAMPLE ENTRY
2026-10-14 09:02Z START. ROE ref AEGIS-2026-001. Window 09:00-17:00 PDT.
2026-10-14 09:05Z Source IP whitelisted with client. Confirmed by J. Doe.
2026-10-14 09:20Z Recon complete: 3 hosts, 11 services, 1 flagged.
2026-10-14 10:41Z Access obtained on WEB-02 as www-data. Impact pending.
2026-10-14 11:15Z Escalated to root on WEB-02. Credentials redacted.
2026-10-14 11:16Z NOTIFIED client contact: database readable by this account.
2026-10-14 11:40Z Cleaned up: no files left, no persistence installed.
2026-10-14 12:00Z END. Evidence archived encrypted. Removal verified.
NOTE
````

Step 2 is the deliverable: 02-preflight.sh — twenty-four checks in six categories that must
run the week before, and it exits non-zero if anything is missing. The note's claim is blunt:
every one of those items has ended a real engagement, and the two most commonly missed are
call the contact before the window, not during it, and IP whitelisting, without which
the client's own IDS files a ticket on you.

03-stop.md is the stop-conditions document, written before the test. The line that matters
most: "A tester who says 'I don't know what happened' after causing it has a much worse problem
than one who says 'I ran X at 14:22 and the service went down at 14:23."
